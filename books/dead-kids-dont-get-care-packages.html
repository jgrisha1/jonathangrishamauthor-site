Dead Kids Don’t Get Care Packages

By Jonathan Grisham

About the Book

Camp Ashknife closed twenty years ago. The official reason was financial. The other reasons were the kind that do not appear in county records, that do not get discussed at the reunion of a camp that never held one, that exist only in the particular silence that settles over a place after the last bus leaves and no one has reason to go back.

Content creators Brandi Loomis and Colt Rader had a reason. The kind that pays. They had permits, equipment, and the particular confidence of people who have never been somewhere that did not want them. They broke in through the east fence. Under a bunk in Cabin 7, they found a care package addressed to Brandi. The return address was her mother's handwriting. The postmark was the summer Brandi was nine. She had never slept in Cabin 7.

By morning, Ashknife's former staff had started arriving for a memorial that none of them had organized. The unopened mail was finding its way out of storage. Each box was addressed to someone. Each box had been waiting.

Every box carries evidence. Every object remembers.

Dead Kids Don't Get Care Packages is Grisham's quiet camp horror: not the kind that announces itself with a hockey mask, but the kind that uses what you already know, what you already own, what you already recognize, to get where it's going.
