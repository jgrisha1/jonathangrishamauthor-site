About the Book

What happens when the Christian home stops being shelter and becomes evidence?

The Christian Home Industry maps the network of churches, Christian schools, Bible colleges, student handbooks, family teachings, dress codes, authority structures, and ministry pipelines that trained families to perform holiness. Obedient children. Modest daughters. Ministry-minded sons. Submissive wives. Firm fathers. Church attendance, school loyalty, Bible college futures. Each piece arranged into proof that the system worked.

This book names the mechanisms behind that image: respectability as doctrine, the K-12 pipeline, the college and church training loop, authority without safe exits, surveillance reframed as discipleship, and the emotional cost paid by the good kid who disappears inside compliance. It also names who paid the cost. Children, wives, queer people, abuse survivors, and questioning believers found themselves with nowhere safe to tell the truth.

Using institutional documents, student handbooks, and careful KJV-based theological reflection, Grisham traces how the "godly home" became a public showroom. Written in a clear layperson's voice, this is not a rejection of home, Scripture, discipline, church, or Christian formation. It is a call to rescue home from the showroom.

Includes a full companion study guide for personal reflection, church discussion, and group use.

Part of a Series

This is Volume 2 of the Harmful Theological Books series. Volume 1, When Obedience Becomes Harm, examines the household theology behind Created to Be His Help Meet and To Train Up a Child, tracing how Scripture can be shaped into a system where visible order is mistaken for spiritual health.
