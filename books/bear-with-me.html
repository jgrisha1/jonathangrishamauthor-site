Bear With Me

By Jonathan Grisham

About the Book

Wonderworld was built for children. The kind of place that smells like popcorn and synthetic vanilla and the low-grade institutional despair of a facility that has never once closed on time. Brenna Bell has worked the floor long enough to know every corner of it: which animatronics stick, which bathrooms flood, which guests will complain about the lines before they ever join one. She has seen everything this place has to offer.

She has not seen this.

Eleven ounces of polyester. Plastic button eyes. A music box mechanism in the chest that plays a twelve-second loop until someone turns the key. The bears are not new. They have been in the gift shop for years, in the prize booth, in the arms of children leaving the park with cotton candy on their shirts and nothing worse than a sunburn. They are not dangerous. There is no mechanism that would allow them to be dangerous. Brenna knows this because she has been assistant manager for six years and there is no version of her job description that includes killer stuffed animals.

One is ridiculous. Ten is a problem. There are a hell of a lot more than ten.

Bear With Me is 101 pages of Grisham's particular brand of horror: funny until it isn't, escalating past the point where you can still call it a joke, and honest about the fact that some situations have no good exit and the people caught in them are just trying to hold the line.
