Religious Trauma Books

Memoir, nonfiction, and horror fiction from inside the wreckage of fundamentalism.

The Independent Fundamental Baptist movement is not a fringe cult you read about in a documentary. It is a network of independent churches across the American South and Midwest that operate with no denominational accountability, no outside oversight, and a theology built on total submission to pastoral authority. In IFB churches, the pastor's word functions as God's word. Questioning leadership is framed as rebellion against the divine. Children are trained toward obedience through fear, and that fear is presented as spiritual formation. I grew up inside that.

What religious trauma does is specific. It is not the same as having a bad experience at church. It is the systematic rewiring of a person's capacity for self-trust, the installation of shame as a default response to independent thought, and the use of eternal consequences to enforce compliance at every level of ordinary life. When the system that is supposed to mediate your relationship with God is also the system surveilling your behavior, controlling your relationships, and deciding what you're allowed to feel, the damage runs deep. It does not leave when you leave. I know this from the inside.

I write about it in multiple registers because the subject demands more than one approach. The memoir is direct testimony. The nonfiction is theological argument. The horror fiction is what happens when I let the emotional truth run loose without the obligation to be fair. All four books below come from the same source: years of living in and then out of a system that mistook control for holiness.

Jonathan Grisham is an IFB survivor, extreme horror author, and lay leader in the United Methodist Church. He lives and writes in East Tennessee. His work covers religious trauma in memoir, nonfiction, and horror fiction because one form is never enough to hold the whole thing.

The fiction does not soft-pedal what religious authoritarian systems do to people. Neither does the memoir. If you are looking for a survivor story that ends in easy redemption, these are the wrong books.
